# Proposal structures by sector

Guidance for shaping proposal content once the profile sector is known. These are defaults; explicit user direction always wins.

## Services and consulting

- Suggested order: cover, executive summary, understanding of the client's situation, scope and deliverables, methodology or approach, project timeline, case studies, pricing, terms, call to action.
- Emphasize the diagnosis: restate the client's problem in one short paragraph before presenting the solution.
- Pricing usually works best as phases or work packages rather than hourly lines.
- Timeline granularity: weeks for engagements under three months, months otherwise.

## Agency and marketing

- Suggested order: cover, executive summary, objectives and KPIs, scope and deliverables, creative or channel approach, case studies, project timeline, pricing, terms, call to action.
- Case studies carry the most weight in this sector; when the profile enables them, place one strong result-oriented case near the front half of the proposal.
- State measurable objectives (reach, leads, conversions) only when the user or Knowledge provides them; never fabricate benchmarks.
- Pricing often mixes a recurring fee and one-off setup lines; keep them as separate items with clear descriptions.

## Software and technology

- Suggested order: cover, executive summary, scope and deliverables, technical approach or architecture summary, project timeline with milestones, about us, pricing, terms, call to action.
- Keep the technical approach short and readable for a non-technical buyer; details belong in an annex or follow-up document.
- Milestone-based timelines pair naturally with milestone-based payment terms.
- Include assumptions and exclusions explicitly (environments, third-party licenses, support boundaries).

## Commerce and products

- Suggested order: cover, executive summary, product or supply description, pricing, delivery timing, terms, about us, call to action.
- Pricing is the center of the document: precise quantities, unit prices, and the exact tax note requested by the user.
- Delivery timing, validity of the offer, and shipping or logistics terms matter more than narrative sections; keep prose minimal.
- Case studies are usually replaced by references or notable clients, and only when documented.

## What a good case-studies section contains

- One to three engagements, each with: client name (only if documented and shareable), starting situation, what was delivered, and one concrete documented result.
- Prefer recent and sector-relevant engagements over older or generic ones.
- Never include confidential figures or client names that Knowledge does not explicitly present as shareable.

## Typical terms labels

Short label and value pairs that fit the renderer terms array: payment schedule, offer validity, delivery timing, exclusions, assumptions, warranty, support window, expenses. Use only the ones relevant to the offer; three to six entries is the usual range.
